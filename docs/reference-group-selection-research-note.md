# Reference-group selection — research note

*Not part of elements/. This records a research programme proposed alongside the lex-345ta / lex-dun6c mint, the part of it that became atoms, the part that did not, and the objections that should be answered before anyone spends time on it. Elements atoms are claims about transferable patterns; a research design is a different artifact class and does not belong in an atom, which is why this is a doc.*

## What became part of elements/

Two atoms, both `under-review` pending procurement:

- **lex-345ta** `adopting-a-reference-groups-norms-before-entry-trades-standing-for-admission` — Merton's anticipatory socialization, minted two-sided: functional for the individual, dysfunctional for the group of membership, and conditional on an open structure. The closed-structure failure (the marginal man) is in the atom.
- **lex-dun6c** `insecure-standing-produces-public-overconformity-that-overshoots-the-groups-baseline` — the display claim. Noel/Wann/Branscombe supply the audience-conditionality, Lakin/Chartrand/Arkin the automaticity, Labov's crossover the cross-domain attestation.

The generalisation the mint makes, which the proposal states more narrowly: the driver is **insecurity of standing**, not being outside the group. Aspirants outside, pledges at the margin, and the rank just below the top all produce the shape. That is what lets one atom carry Merton's outsiders and Noel's peripheral insiders and Labov's second-highest status group without straining.

Placing it against `lex-7ra5h countersignaling` was the other structural payoff: over-signalling and under-signalling are the two tails of one distribution over security-of-position. Neither atom reads a group's norms correctly on its own.

## What did not become part of elements/, and why

The research programme — the reference-group-selection gap, the cross-server observability argument, the peak-then-relax predictions, the falsifier list. All of it is a design for finding something out, not a claim about a transferable pattern. It is recorded here.

## The programme as proposed

**The gap.** Merton flagged the determinants of reference-group selection as the open problem and it stayed open. Given several candidate groups, what decides where an aspirant aims? Incumbent answers come from social-movement research — network ties, structural availability — and give the aspirant little agency.

**Why now.** The choice set has never been observable. Persistent cross-server user IDs make a single user's trajectory across many otherwise independent communities visible, with sampling, partial participation and commitment all timestamped.

**Predictions.** Anticipatory signalling produces a peak-then-relax curve: marker adoption rises steeply before admission, overshoots the community mean, relaxes after. Ordinary socialization produces a monotonic rise after entry. Aspirants should be more orthodox than members.

**Falsifiers as proposed.** No pre-entry adoption (socialization wins). Overshoot that never relaxes (internalisation, not signalling). Identical curves for people never admitted (exposure, not aspiration). Convergence explained by prior ties or inbound recruitment (the sociologists win).

**Explicitly dropped.** The ROI/strategic-chooser framing, on the grounds that post-hoc rationalisation does too much of its work. The medium/cheap-signal argument, parked rather than refuted.

## Objections to answer first

**1. The no-agent commitment contradicts the research question.** This is the serious one. The question is *what decides where an aspirant aims* — a question about selection among alternatives, which is the shape of a choice. The programme then drops the strategic-chooser framing because the theory is "stronger without an agent," and simultaneously faults the incumbent structural answers for giving the aspirant "almost no agency." You cannot have all three. Either agency is doing work in the selection story, in which case something like the dropped ROI framing has to come back in a defensible form; or it is not, in which case network ties and structural availability are not a deficiency to be corrected, they are the answer, and the contribution is measuring them at a resolution nobody could before. The second option is a perfectly good paper and it is the one the data actually supports. Pick it deliberately rather than inheriting the tension.

**2. "Largely nonconscious, so participants can't report it and shouldn't be asked" is a scope condition doing the work of a result.** Only the mimicry evidence establishes nonconsciousness. Audience-conditional behaviour need not be unconscious to go unreported. Ruling self-report inadmissible in advance removes the cheapest available disconfirmation and makes the pattern unfalsifiable by construction — which is a heavier cost than whatever noise self-report would have introduced. Demote it to a testable sub-claim: ask people, treat a clean report as evidence, and treat a systematic report/behaviour mismatch as the interesting finding.

**3. The overshoot needs a contemporaneous baseline, not a fixed one.** "Overshoots the community mean" is only meaningful against the mean *at that moment*, measured on the people actually present. Communities drift, and often fastest during the growth phase when new arrivals are most numerous — which is exactly the window the design samples. A flat individual trajectory measured against a stale mean produces a textbook overshoot artifact. Cohort-relative measurement, or the finding is not safe.

**4. The admitted/never-admitted comparison is not a control.** It is offered as the falsifier for exposure-rather-than-aspiration, but the two groups are separated by the very outcome the overshoot is supposed to explain: if overshooting causes admission, then conditioning on admission conditions on the treatment. This is the same identification problem that limits the American Soldier evidence, and better data does not dissolve it. It needs a design answer — a discontinuity in admission rules, a capacity constraint, a queue, some source of variation in admission that is not the behaviour itself.

**5. Survivorship in the relax phase.** Overshooters who are admitted stay observable; overshooters who are refused leave, often silently. The post-admission relaxation may be attrition rather than relaxation. Whether departures are observable at all in cross-server data is a question to settle before the curve is interpreted.

**6. Consent and data governance, at design time rather than at review time.** Linking a person's trajectory across communities they joined separately reconstructs something none of those communities holds on its own, and the design specifically identifies individuals by inferred aspiration and insecure standing — an inference people have not volunteered and would often object to having drawn. Public-API availability is not consent, and cross-server IDs being technically persistent is not a licence to link them. This will gate publication and it is far cheaper to answer now: what is the unit of analysis, is aggregate sufficient, can the linkage be one-way hashed, is there a route through the platforms rather than around them.

## Assessment

Objections 1 and 2 are about the theory and should be resolved before any data is touched; both are resolvable by choosing the weaker, better-supported version of the claim. Objections 3 through 5 are design problems with known fixes, and 4 is the one that decides whether the result will convince anyone. Objection 6 is a gate, not a critique.

The underlying observation — that the choice set is newly observable and that this is the thing that kept Merton's question open for seventy years — is correct and is the strongest part of the proposal. It survives all six objections intact.
